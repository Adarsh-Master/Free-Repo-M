Date: 05-Sep-2026 | Time: 9:00 AM - 1:00 PM

Activity: Deep Dive into the Biology of Decay
Notes:
Yesterday's visit changed our perspective. If seeing the rot is too late, how do we catch it earlier?
Spent 4 hours reading biological research papers on post-harvest crop decay.
Scientific Findings:

When fresh produce starts to spoil due to trapped heat or moisture, it undergoes Anaerobic Fermentation.

Formula: C6H12O6 (Glucose) -> 2 C2H5OH (Ethanol) + 2 CO2 (Carbon Dioxide) + Heat

The rotting crops release Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs), primarily ethanol gas.
THE BREAKTHROUGH: According to research, this chemical off-gassing happens approximately 28 to 36 hours BEFORE any physical black/brown spots appear on the crop's skin.
This is the hidden window of opportunity!

Date: 10-Sep-2026 | Time: 4:30 PM - 6:30 PM
Location: School ATL Lab
Activity: Scalability Math (How many sensors are needed?)
Notes:
Sat with Ranveer to do the math. If a godown is 5,000 sq ft, how many Rs. 850 sensors do they actually need?
[Rough scribbles]
Radius of MQ-3 = 5 meters? (Crossed out - No, in a closed indoor warehouse aisle with stacked sacks, airflow is restricted).
Let's assume a practical effective radius of 2 to 2.5 meters.
Area covered = pi * r^2 = 3.14 * (2.2)^2 = approx 15.2 sq meters.
Converted to feet: 15.2 * 10.76 = ~163 sq ft. Let's round down to a safe 150 sq ft per node.
A standard 50kg burlap sack takes about 1.5 sq ft of floor/rack space.
Calculation: 150 sq ft / 1.5 sq ft = 100.
Conclusion: Just 1 Sensor Node can monitor a dense cluster of 80 to 100 sacks! A massive godown would only need a small network of these, making the ROI (Return on Investment) extremely high.

MAJOR SETBACK:
The MQ-3 is reading gas perfectly, but the DHT11 is throwing continuous "NaN" (Not a Number) errors.
Troubleshooting: Checked VCC and GND. Tried adding a pull-up resistor. Nothing worked.
Technical flaw discovered: The DHT11 is an extremely slow sensor (requires 2 full seconds between reads). This timing delay is clashing with the fast analog reads we need for the gas sensor. Furthermore, this specific DHT11 module seems to have a burnt internal thermistor.

Date: 13-Sep-2026 | Time: 11:30 AM - 1:00 PM
Location: School IT Lab
Activity: Strategic Pivot (Removing the DHT11)
Notes:
Discussed the DHT11 failure with Sonika Ma'am. We realized something important: Do we really need the temperature sensor?
If the biological fact is that ethanol gas (VOC) is the direct byproduct of the rotting fermentation, then the gas level itself is the ultimate indicator. Temperature and humidity are just secondary environmental factors that speed it up.
Decision: We are removing the DHT11 entirely. It is slowing down our processing loop and complicating the hardware for very little added value.
By dropping it, we make the edge-node cheaper, faster, and less prone to hardware failure. In the future, if a massive industrial warehouse needs it, we can always integrate advanced robotics or high-end BME280 sensors, but for this MVP (Minimum Viable Product), a single highly-calibrated MQ-3 sensor is superior.

Activity: Threshold Calibration & Emergency Coding
Notes:
Now that it is a 1-sensor system, the MQ-3 needs to be perfectly calibrated.
Wrote a clean C++ code for Arduino.
[Scribble rough logic]
int gasVal = analogRead(A0);
if (gasVal > threshold) { trigger buzzer; }
How do we find the threshold without rotting real vegetables for 4 days?
Calibration Test:

Measured normal ambient room air: Values fluctuated between 90 and 120. (Baseline ~105).

Took an alcohol-based hand sanitizer (simulates ethanol off-gassing). Placed it 10cm from the sensor.

Values instantly spiked to 650 - 750!
Math for Threshold:
Target = Baseline + [ (Spike - Baseline) * 0.35 ] -> 35% buffer to prevent false alarms from floor cleaners.
Target = 105 + (600 * 0.35) = 105 + 210 = 315.
Let's round down to 300 for faster early-warning.
Set const int gasThreshold = 300; in the code.
Tested it again. The moment the sanitizer gets close, the red LED flashes and the buzzer screams. The hardware Phase 1 is officially successful!

Date: 16-Sep-2026 | Time: 4:30 PM - 6:00 PM
Location: School IT Lab
Activity: The False Alarm Problem & The AI Idea
Notes:
Demonstrated the Phase 1 hardware to Sonika Ma'am. The buzzer worked perfectly when we brought the sanitizer close. But Ma'am asked a very smart question: "What if a warehouse worker is just cleaning the floor with phenyl or alcohol? Won't the alarm ring for no reason?"
She is right. A simple hardware `if (gas > 300)` threshold is blind. It will cause "False Alarms".
Brainstorming with Ranveer: How do we fix this? 
The difference between floor cleaner and rotting food is TIME. Floor cleaner spikes the gas for 5 minutes and evaporates. Rotting food causes a *continuous, steady rise* in gas over 24+ hours.
Decision: We need an Artificial Intelligence (Machine Learning) model. The AI shouldn't just look at the current gas level; it needs to analyze the "Moving Average" (historical trend) to predict real spoilage risk!
